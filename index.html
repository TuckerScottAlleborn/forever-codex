Forever Codex

A codex for veterans coming back to Azeroth: what’s different, set beside the Vanilla they remember.

Forever isn’t Vanilla again, and it isn’t Retail rewound. It keeps the old world, its rules and its history, then changes them on purpose. Each book reads one part of the game against Vanilla: the story, the classes, the races, the world around them, from dungeons and raids to professions and PvP, and last the companion pets. A patch log, a glossary and the common questions sit at the back.

vanilla, then forever

What’s different, area by area

Nine places where a veteran’s memory will mislead, each with the book that sets it straight.

  1. The story

    book i · the lore →
    in vanilla

    One road ahead: Molten Core, then the Dark Portal, Outland and Northrend.

    →
    in forever

    Its own time bubble from early Year 1. The old history stands, and what came after it in Retail is precedent, not destiny.

  2. The world

    book iv · systems →
    in vanilla

    Closed gates and unfinished roads: Hyjal off-limits, Dalaran behind its barrier, Alcaz Prison shut, and everything kept per character.

    →
    in forever

    Four new regions (Hyjal, Riverglades, Shen’dralas and Zephras Isle), over a thousand new quests, account-wide Legacy and optional transmog. Still no flying, and no level scaling.

  3. Dungeons and raids

    book iv · dungeons and raids →
    in vanilla

    The Classic dungeon set, then 40-player Molten Core and Onyxia, found through chat and meeting stones.

    →
    in forever

    Nine new dungeons from level 13 to 60, a raid tier for 10, 20 and 40 players on December 9, and an LFG listing tool.

  4. Gear and stats

    book iv · gear and stats →
    in vanilla

    Separate physical and spell hit and crit, weapon skill as the melee stat, and dungeon blues of very uneven worth.

    →
    in forever

    One Hit and one Crit stat, weapon skill kept but smaller on gear, a new Expertise stat, and every dungeon drop re-examined.

  5. The classes

    book ii · the classes →
    in vanilla

    Specs that barely did their advertised job, party-only buffs, and a respec bill for every hybrid.

    →
    in forever

    Complete specs (Seal of Fury taunts, Arcane Blast builds an engine, Survival fights in melee), raid-wide buffs, dual spec at 40 and a 16-point milestone.

  6. The races

    book iii · the races →
    in vanilla

    Racial weapon skill and resistances decided it: Humans for swords, Dwarves for Fear Ward, Gnomes for Intellect.

    →
    in forever

    Racial weapon skill is gone and every Priest learns Fear Ward. Each race carries a kind of power, with Dwarf Shaman, Human Hunter, Gnome Priest and the new Skyborne.

  7. Professions

    book iv · professions →
    in vanilla

    A Classic catalogue heavy on weak leveling crafts.

    →
    in forever

    Over 600 new recipes, crafted leveling sets, campsites every profession builds into, and recipes bought with Merchant’s Favor.

  8. PvP

    book iv · pvp →
    in vanilla

    A weekly standings race to Grand Marshal, in three battlegrounds.

    →
    in forever

    Ranks 1–14 earned with Rank Points, Honor to spend, a seasonal Field of Honor Journey, and Darkspear Islands, a new 15v15 battleground.

  9. The companions

    book v · the companions →
    in vanilla

    Bag-space vanity items, a few brutal world drops, and the Collector’s Edition trio out of reach for good.

    →
    in forever

    Learned, account-wide companions, new ones from rares, quests, emotes, Legacy and reputation, a Black Market Auction House, and the Zergling, Panda Cub and Mini Diablo sold again.

The codex covers the Alliance side. The patch log dates every change it draws on, and the appendix lists what’s still unknown before launch.

coming back as a

Warrior

Distance from Vanilla: Low–moderate

still holds

Rage, stances, weapon swings and threat all still need managing. Of the nine classes, Warrior changes least in concept.

changed most

  • Rage from damage taken is counted before armor and absorbs, so better gear or a Priest’s shield no longer starves it.
  • Tactical Mastery is baseline at 14, and Victory Rush at 20 heals 10% of max health after a kill.
  • Recklessness, Retaliation and Shield Wall stop sharing a cooldown, and Battle Shout is raid-wide.
  • Arms’ Weaponmaster replaces the old weapon specializations, so no weapon type is the wrong one.

relearn first

Arms around Overpower, Rend and Slam, with Rend enabling Overpower; Fury around Raging Blows, a better dual-wield Whirlwind and cheaper Cleave.

Paladin

Distance from Vanilla: Very high

still holds

Slow, mana-hungry, seal-and-judgment Paladin.

changed most

  • Judgment no longer consumes the Seal, ending the judge-and-reseal routine.
  • Holy Strike comes early, and Consecration is baseline at 20, at full strength on the first four targets.
  • Protection’s Seal of Fury has a Judgment that taunts: an Alliance Paladin finally has a real taunt.
  • Salvation no longer takes a Blessing slot.

relearn first

Retribution’s Twist of Light makes seal twisting a talent, and Holy now runs on Holy Shock, with Light’s Vigil at 31.

coming back as a

Hunter

Distance from Vanilla: High

still holds

Old-school Hunter rules: the dead zone, half a second standing still for Auto Shot, and shot timing.

changed most

  • Every Hunter learns Aimed Shot at 20, sharing a cooldown with Multi-Shot, not Arcane Shot.
  • Traps work in combat, with one Fire and one Ice trap active at once.
  • Pets inherit stats from the Hunter’s gear, and every family has its own utility ability.
  • Survival is a melee and ranged hybrid; Marksmanship’s Lone Wolf rewards leaving the pet behind.

relearn first

Pet family choice, which matters more than in Vanilla, and Survival’s close-range play around Mongoose Bite and Strider Kick.

Rogue

Distance from Vanilla: Moderate

still holds

Energy, combo points, positionals, poisons, stealth and control.

changed most

  • Poisons carry more charges and stack with sharpening and weight stones.
  • Sap no longer breaks Stealth, and one-handed axes are usable.
  • Expose Armor equals five Sunders and no longer blocks Sunder Armor.
  • Assassination becomes the dagger and poison spec, with Mutilate at 21 and the Venom finisher at 31.

relearn first

Assassination’s upkeep of Slice and Dice and Venom, and Subtlety’s Hemorrhage, which now feeds the Rogue’s own Rupture.

Priest

Distance from Vanilla: High

still holds

Classic mana management and cast speed.

changed most

  • Fear Ward and Devouring Plague are for every Priest, and Divine Spirit and Shadow Word: Death are baseline.
  • Discipline is a shield-healing and damage-weaving spec, built on Penance and Divine Aegis.
  • Holy gains Binding Heal and Prayer of Mending; Lightwell is gone.
  • Shadow Weaving is a personal buff, and Vampiric Embrace an activated cooldown.

relearn first

Discipline, the spec veterans most need to relearn: Power in Light ties Smite and Penance to targets carrying Holy Fire or Shadow Word: Pain.

Shaman

Distance from Vanilla: Very high

still holds

Totems still get placed and thought about.

changed most

  • New to the Alliance, as Dwarf Shaman.
  • Totems use a one-second global cooldown, buff totems last five minutes, and three Call presets drop a whole set.
  • Grace of Air, Strength of Earth, Windfury and Flametongue are raid-wide.
  • Elemental gets Lava Burst and Elemental Mastery, Enhancement Maelstrom Weapon, Restoration Riptide.

relearn first

The totem bar itself, even for anyone who has played a Shaman before: Totemic Recall, Totemic Projection and the presets replace the four-global totem piano.

coming back as a

Mage

Distance from Vanilla: High

still holds

The Classic control chassis: Blink, Polymorph, Counterspell, Frost Nova and Classic casting.

changed most

  • Frostfire Bolt at 40 counts as both Fire and Frost.
  • Rank 1 Frostbolt is penalized; Frost Blast at 24 keeps its slow.
  • Arcane is a real damage spec: Arcane Blast stacks spell power against a rising mana cost.
  • Fire runs on Heating Up and Pyroblast; Frost on Fingers of Frost, Ice Lance and a redesigned Winter’s Chill.

relearn first

Big pulls: Improved Blizzard slows less, and Cone of Cold’s full control reaches only the first four targets.

coming back as a

Warlock

Distance from Vanilla: High

still holds

The Classic Warlock toolbox. Vanilla Warlock already worked.

changed most

  • Curse of Agony and Curse of Doom become Banes, separate from utility Curses; Curse of Shadow is gone.
  • Life Tap scales with Spirit, and Firestones and Spellstones are imbues that stack with oils.
  • Demonology’s Demonic Pact keeps one demon’s sacrifice bonus with another demon out.
  • Destruction gets Incinerate as a real Fire filler, and Bane of Havoc for two-target cleave.

relearn first

Where each tree ends: Affliction in Wrack and critting DoTs, Demonology with the demon, Destruction in Immolate and Incinerate.

coming back as a

Druid

Distance from Vanilla: Very high

still holds

Vanilla Druid: forms, HoTs, Healing Touch and slow-cast Balance.

changed most

  • Shapeshifted damage scales with the weapon’s DPS, and weapon effects work in forms.
  • Tiger’s Fury is replaced by Shifting Power, which turns base mana into Energy: powershifting as a talent.
  • Omen of Clarity is baseline at 20, Barkskin works in forms, and Druids get an out-of-combat Revive.
  • Swiftmend no longer consumes its HoT, and Wild Growth sits at 31.

relearn first

Feral around Primal Bite and Berserk, and Balance’s Eclipse, where Wrath shortens the next Starfires.

coming back as a

Human

still holds

Perception, for stealth detection.

changed most

  • Sword and Mace Specialization become Ruthless: +1% crit with any weapon.
  • The Human Spirit doubles to +10% Spirit.
  • Diplomacy is gone: no automatic +10% reputation.
  • Will to Survive, a new stun break.

relearn first

No longer the default melee race: now the broad PvP pick, and a coherent Spirit-healing Priest.

Dwarf

still holds

Stoneform, now the cleanest defensive cooldown on the Alliance.

changed most

  • Gun Specialization and Frost Resistance are gone.
  • Artisan’s Precision: +1% hit with spells and attacks.
  • Stoneform cuts Physical damage by 10% instead of adding armor.
  • Big Game Hunter adds 5% damage against Beasts, and Find Treasure can turn up extra chest loot.

relearn first

No longer the Fear Ward race, and now the only Alliance Shaman race.

Night Elf

still holds

Shadowmeld, and Quickness’s dodge.

changed most

  • Elune’s Light: +10% crit for 15 seconds, every 3 minutes.
  • Shadowmeld works in combat, like Fade rather than Vanish.
  • Quickness adds 2% movement speed, and Wisp Spirit rises to +75%.
  • Nature Resistance is gone.

relearn first

Combat Shadowmeld opens no Stealth-only abilities: there’s no Retail-style restealth.

coming back as a

Gnome

still holds

Escape Artist, for roots and snares.

changed most

  • Expansive Mind: +5% maximum Mana, Energy or Rage, not +5% Intellect.
  • Eureka!: 8 seconds of cheaper, stronger damaging abilities, every 2 minutes.
  • Escape Artist is instant, with 3 seconds of immunity.
  • Engineering Specialization means 20% fewer device failures, not +15 skill; Arcane Resistance is gone.

relearn first

Gnome Priest is new, with Confounding Flash and Contingency Plan, and among the strongest-looking Alliance Priests.

coming back as a

Skyborne

High Order Skyborne, new in Forever

who they are

An entirely new race: descendants of Highborne rebels who fled Eldre’Thalas after the War of the Ancients. The High Order side with the Alliance.

what they bring

  • Wind-Blessed: +2% haste, always on.
  • Read Leyline: regeneration anywhere, doubled once Attuned at a Ley Line.
  • Walk on Air: a 10-second glide, every 90 seconds.
  • Elemental Insight: +5% damage against Elementals.

worth knowing

Playing one needs the Skyborne Heroic Pack or higher, per Blizzard. Its racials are also the ones whose level-60 value most needs testing after launch.
