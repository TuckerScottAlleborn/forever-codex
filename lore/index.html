The Lore

History up to Year 1 is foundation. Everything after it is precedent, not destiny.

Forever isn’t Vanilla again, and it isn’t retail rewound. Blizzard is treating it as a new, permanent branch of Warcraft continuity that lives in its own “time bubble”: the established history stays, and from original WoW onward, events are free to unfold differently.

So this book comes in three parts: the history that’s already written, the page one where Forever picks up the story, and the pages still unwritten.

  1. I

    ~10,000 years ago Foundation

    Titans, Sargeras and the War of the Ancients

    Long before the Alliance or the Horde, Azeroth sits in a cosmos shaped by powers such as the Titans. One of them, Sargeras, turns against his former purpose and becomes master of the Burning Legion.

    The Legion’s invasion, roughly ten thousand years before modern Warcraft, culminates in the War of the Ancients: the catastrophe underneath most of night-elven history, and the source of the lasting fault lines between night elves and Highborne.

    canon checkOversimplified

    “The Titans created Azeroth.”

    Blizzard describes them as great cosmic powers that shape and order worlds. “Shaped and ordered” is safer than “created everything.”

    forever adds a branch here

    Highborne rebels who fled Eldre’Thalas after the war eventually become the Skyborne. More on page one.

  2. II

    The First War Foundation

    The Dark Portal

    Medivh, Guardian of Tirisfal but corrupted through Sargeras, conspires with the orc warlock Gul’dan to open the Dark Portal between Azeroth and Draenor. The Horde that comes through has itself been transformed by demonic influence.

    Stormwind eventually falls. Garona assassinates King Llane, and Anduin Lothar leads the survivors north.

  3. III

    The Second War Foundation

    The Alliance of Lordaeron

    Lothar convinces King Terenas Menethil II and the other human kingdoms that the Horde is an existential threat, and the Alliance of Lordaeron forms with dwarven and high-elven support. Gul’dan abandons the Horde’s war for his own ambitions, and the Alliance recovers and wins.

    Lothar dies fighting Orgrim Doomhammer, Turalyon rallies the Alliance, and many of the surviving orcs end up in internment camps. Then the Alliance itself begins to fracture.

    canon checkHolds

    The standard account of the First and Second Wars.

    It stands. Nothing to update.

  4. IV

    The Third War Foundation

    Arthas, Thrall and the Legion

    Thrall, a liberated orc who rejects the Horde’s demonic past, frees other orcs from internment and builds a new Horde in Kalimdor. Jaina Proudmoore, a gifted human mage, becomes one of the leaders willing to work across faction lines when a greater threat appears.

    Arthas Menethil, Prince of Lordaeron and a paladin, chases the plague of undeath through an increasingly ruthless campaign, takes up Frostmourne and falls into the Lich King’s service. He destroys Lordaeron and devastates Quel’Thalas. Among his victims is Sylvanas Windrunner, Ranger-General of Silvermoon, whom he kills and raises into undeath. She later breaks free of the Scourge and leads the Forsaken: undead who have their own will back.

    Meanwhile the Burning Legion invades Kalimdor. Tyrande Whisperwind and Malfurion Stormrage lead the night-elven resistance, and Illidan Stormrage, imprisoned for millennia, is released in the emergency. Horde, human and night-elf forces fight together against Archimonde’s advance, and Archimonde is defeated at Mount Hyjal.

    canon checkSettled

    “Forever could change how Hyjal went.”

    It can’t. Archimonde’s defeat is already part of Forever’s history, and Forever explores what came after it. The branch comes later.

  5. V

    After the Third War Foundation

    The settlement

    The familiar map emerges. Thrall’s Horde establishes itself in Kalimdor, Jaina’s people found Theramore, the night elves recover from Hyjal, and Stormwind remains the main human power in the south.

    Northern Lordaeron becomes a battlefield for Scourge remnants, the Forsaken, the Scarlet Crusade and others.

  6. VI

    The four-year gap Bridge

    Forsaken Kingdom

    Warcraft III Reforged: Forsaken Kingdom picks up after Arthas returns to Lordaeron and fills the four years before WoW, following Sylvanas’s Forsaken as they establish Undercity and join the Horde. Its two new leads are Garek Bandarion, a newly risen Forsaken warrior, and Dark Ranger Anya. It covers:

    • how Undercity was created
    • how Forsaken society took shape
    • why the Scourge didn’t simply take Lordaeron back
    • how the Forsaken came to join the Horde
    • where Forsaken Paladins come from, a storyline that continues directly into Forever

    canon checkUpdated

    “Frozen Throne Sylvanas, then straight to Vanilla Undercity.”

    That gap is filled now. Forsaken Kingdom is the direct prelude to Forever.

That’s the written record. The page turns here.

part two

Page One

Year 1: where Forever picks up the story, and what’s waiting on the ground when it does.

early Year 1 Forever canon

The starting line

Forever begins after Forsaken Kingdom, before Molten Core, and before Kazzak reopens the Dark Portal: early Year 1, before later journeys such as Northrend reshape the story.

So it inherits original Azeroth’s unresolved world, not its later answers. Its launch world heads for the unfinished-looking corners of the old map: Lordaeron, Dalaran, Alcaz Island, Un’Goro, Azshara and more. The big new threads:

  • Hyjal, in the aftermath of its legendary battle.
  • The Shen’dralar and Eldre’Thalas, given more attention.
  • Riverglades, opening territory between familiar Eastern Kingdoms regions.
  • Forsaken Paladins, pursuing the Light while their own society distrusts them.
  • The Skyborne, arriving from a civilization that has been hidden from the world.

The Skyborne, briefly

Shen’dorei means “hidden people.” They descend from Highborne rebels who fled Eldre’Thalas after the War of the Ancients, found refuge tied to Skywall and its elemental wind spirits, and built a civilization of their own.

Now their wind patrons have disappeared and their home is failing. At character creation, the Windshapers side with the Horde and the High Order with the Alliance.

in books ii and iii

Alliance Skyborne can be Warriors, Hunters, Rogues, Druids and Mages, with always-on haste and a glide of their own. Their racials, in the races book.

canon checkNew canon

“They aren’t in the old lore, so they’re a retcon.”

No contradiction inside Forever: they’re official Forever lore. Their place in modern retail continuity is unspecified.

part three

Still Unwritten

Where the story could go from here. Everything in this part is inference, unless it’s marked as announced.

Soft spoilers

These guesses build on Blizzard’s dungeon and raid previews, and any of them could turn out right. Anyone going in blind can skip ahead to the epilogue.

before guessing

Later Warcraft knowledge helps explain these characters, but it shouldn’t be quietly imported into Forever as future canon. That’s the continuity boundary, and Blizzard has deliberately left room to surprise players.

Three things not to assume

  1. Doesn’t hold

    “Forever is the Vanilla timeline”

    the shorthand
    “TBC and Wrath are coming. We know how this goes.”
    canon
    Forever starts in early Year 1, but in its own time bubble.

    Later retail outcomes aren’t predetermined.

  2. Unspecified

    “Outland comes next”

    the shorthand
    “The Dark Portal reopens and we head to Outland.”
    canon
    Forever begins before Kazzak reopens the Dark Portal, and Blizzard hasn’t committed it to replaying TBC.

    Open. Nothing has been announced.

  3. Memory, not prophecy

    Arthas, Illidan and the later expansions

    the shorthand
    “We know where Arthas and Illidan end up.”
    canon
    Their histories before Forever still matter, and Arthas’s path has led him toward the Lich King. But Blizzard hasn’t announced that Forever must reproduce Wrath of the Lich King, or any of their later retail endpoints.

    Their Forever futures are unspecified.

The open quests

Blizzard wants Forever’s stories to feel as if they always existed behind Classic’s closed gates, unfinished roads and background mysteries. That suggests a shape: start with the questions players could physically see in old Azeroth, let local stories interlock, then reveal that some of them are pieces of something larger. Each hook below starts from what’s been announced.

  1. The Stormwind Conspiracy

    (Dungeon)

    Varian, Onyxia, the Defias and the naga turn out to be one larger political mystery.

    Announced Forever canon

    Alcaz Prison deliberately calls back the old rumors that high-ranking prisoners, King Varian Wrynn among them, passed through Alcaz. Inside, the Defias and the naga are fighting each other. Meanwhile, Onyxia’s Lair is a launch-tier raid.

    Why it’s a hook

    Two of Vanilla’s most famous Stormwind mysteries are being put in the spotlight at the same time.

    Where it could lead Inference

    Stormwind’s legitimacy, its noble politics and the Defias grievance could get a more coherent arc than Vanilla ever gave them.

    ?Still unknown

    Varian’s exact situation. The wording is rumor, not confirmation that he’s sitting in a cell.

  2. The Barrow Deeps

    (Raid)

    Illidan’s old prison reopens, and brings a night-elven reckoning with it.

    Announced Forever canon

    Barrow Deeps is the underground complex tied to Illidan’s ten-thousand-year imprisonment, with entrances linked to Hyjal, Winterspring and Blackmaw Hold. It’s one of the three raids scheduled for December 9.

    Why it’s a hook

    The place is bound up with Tyrande, Malfurion and Illidan, and with how the night elves treat dangerous magic.

    Where it could lead Inference

    Forever can ask whether locking Illidan away was a single moral failure, or a sign of deeper divisions in kaldorei society.

    ?Still unknown

    What is actually awakening in the Deeps, and whether Illidan himself comes back into the story.

  3. Rebuilding Hyjal

    (Raid)

    Hyjal becomes a reconstruction story, not just sacred scenery.

    Announced Forever canon

    Forever visits Hyjal in the aftermath of Archimonde’s defeat. Hyjal Summit is a substantial 20-player raid with 13 bosses.

    Why it’s a hook

    The Third War saved the world and wrecked the heart of night-elven civilization.

    Where it could lead Inference

    Druids, priestesses, ancients and outsiders could split over how to rebuild, who gets access to Hyjal, and how much arcane power should be tolerated again.

    ?Still unknown

    How any of it resolves politically.

  4. Two Highborne Answers

    The Shen’dralar and the Skyborne give competing answers to the Highborne question.

    Announced Forever canon

    Eldre’Thalas, old Dire Maul, holds one surviving Highborne tradition: the Shen’dralar. Forever adds a second branch, the Skyborne, whose ancestors fled Eldre’Thalas and built a civilization around the wind, away from Azeroth. Their Windshapers join the Horde, their High Order the Alliance.

    Why it’s a hook

    They’re perfect mirrors: the same ancestry, and radically different ways of surviving.

    Where it could lead Inference

    The night elves may have to rethink ancient prejudices, and Alliance and Horde recruitment of rival Skyborne movements turns an old elven dispute into present-day faction politics.

    ?Still unknown

    How politically important the Skyborne end up being.

  5. The Light Among the Forsaken

    Forsaken Paladins start a fight over what “Forsaken” means.

    Announced Forever canon

    Their story continues from Forsaken Kingdom into Forever and centers on Bandarion Keep. These Light-wielding undead are mistrusted even by other Forsaken.

    Why it’s a hook

    Nothing attacks the old belief that the Light has abandoned the Forsaken more directly than undead who can wield it.

    Where it could lead Inference

    Friction with Sylvanas’s leadership, the Scarlet Crusade, the surviving paladin traditions and ordinary Forsaken.

    ?Still unknown

    Whether it ends in reconciliation, persecution or schism.

  6. Farther Below Azeroth

    (Dungeon)

    The underground clues are converging on something bigger.

    Announced Forever canon

    Corruption is spreading through Blackmaw Hold, a furbolg city beneath Azshara, and Blizzard’s description teases a route “farther below Azeroth.” Barrow Deeps connects to Blackmaw. Shaper’s Terrace is an ancient facility in Un’Goro, a zone that has always been full of Titan mysteries.

    Why it’s a hook

    Blizzard is laying a trail across the map, not building isolated instances.

    Where it could lead Inference

    A grounded Classic adventure could open onto Titan, elemental or other ancient forces without jumping straight to retail’s cosmic scale.

    ?Still unknown

    The culprit. Naming an Old God, a Titan keeper or the Void right now is theory.

  7. The Violet City

    (Dungeon)

    Dalaran comes back early, as a contested magical power.

    Announced Forever canon

    The new City of Dalaran dungeon is set in Alterac. Players get in after earning the Kirin Tor’s trust, and the city’s old magical situation is disrupted: its barrier is down.

    Why it’s a hook

    Dalaran sits, geographically and politically, between Alliance interests, Forsaken Lordaeron and Scourge territory.

    Where it could lead Inference

    The Kirin Tor could become a real regional power instead of waiting around for Northrend.

    ?Still unknown

    Kael’thas, the high elves and any blood-elf future are tempting connections. Their Forever paths are unspecified.

  8. The Portal Stays Shut

    The Dark Portal stays closed long enough for Azeroth itself to be the protagonist.

    Announced Forever canon

    Blizzard keeps describing Azeroth and exploration as central to Forever, and it starts the branch before Kazzak reopens the Portal.

    Why it’s a hook

    Replaying TBC right away would undercut the whole premise: finding more inside the original continents.

    Where it could lead Inference

    Illidan, Quel’Thalas, Kael’thas and Outland could return in radically different contexts, or not at all.

    ?Still unknown

    Whether and when the Dark Portal reopens. Of all the big questions, this one is the most open.

the big picture Inference

Put together, these hooks suggest Forever’s most interesting possibility isn’t one shocking retcon. It’s causal divergence: familiar people start in familiar places, but Vanilla’s half-told stories resolve differently, and the Azeroth that results slowly stops lining up with the expansions veterans remember.

epilogue

How to file old knowledge

Warcraft has an intimidating amount of lore, but Forever’s canon boundary is unusually simple. Every piece of remembered lore lands in one of three places.

  1. Foundation

    Useful background

    Everything that establishes the world, through the settlement after Warcraft III.

  2. Bridge

    Essential

    Forsaken Kingdom, Forever’s own connective tissue.

  3. Another continuity

    Precedent, not destiny

    Anything from later expansions. It belongs under “what happened in another Warcraft continuity after this point” until Forever itself confirms it.
