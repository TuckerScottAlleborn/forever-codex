The World

Evolution, not replacement: the old world stands, and Forever builds beside it.

Forever keeps Classic’s pace. Outdoor mobs still take 10–15 seconds to kill, the world doesn’t scale to the player, and there’s no flying. The old dungeons keep their maps, bosses and keys. What changes is how much there is to do, and the systems around it: nine new dungeons, more than a thousand new quests, over 600 new recipes, an LFG listing tool, account-wide Legacy and optional transmog.

This book covers dungeons and raids, gear and stats, professions, the systems around them, what costs money, and PvP, all on the Alliance side. Forever launches November 4, so most of it comes from Blizzard’s previews and the beta. Each unknown is marked as unknown rather than filled in from Classic.

finding a group

Gear and stats

Blizzard’s aim is to unsolve gearing without abandoning Classic’s item system.

correctionChanged

“Weapon skill is gone in Forever.”

It isn’t. Blizzard says weapon skill still works; gear just gives less of it. What the October 26 pass removed were the racial weapon specializations. A new Expertise stat now cuts enemy dodge and parry, alongside weapon skill, not instead of it.

yesThe beta’s known-issues notes discuss their chance and damage penalty.

unknownForever’s skill and itemization changes mean Classic’s number shouldn’t be assumed.

And 16% for spells?

unknownOne Hit stat now covers spells, and Blizzard hasn’t published the level-63 formula.

Does dual wield still miss more?

yesTalents such as Warrior’s Furious Precision add off-hand hit. The exact threshold is unknown.

Is there crit suppression?

unknownNo raid-boss table yet. The new character panel does explain hit, crit and crit immunity in its tooltips.

Itemization. Every dungeon drop was reviewed and unique boss items improved; world-drop epics are being made more worthwhile, and new items use conditional effects, creature-type bonuses and active effects. Random-suffix gear such as “of the Bear” stays. Because one Hit or Crit piece now serves both melee and casters, and healing gear adds damage, gear valuation changes most for Retribution, Feral, Balance, Enhancement and Shadow. New Season One tier sets, built around Forever’s specs, are in the data but still subject to change.

Loot. There’s no switch to personal loot. Transmog changes one thing: when an uncommon or rare dungeon item drops, eligible group members get its appearance without taking the item, while an epic raid appearance goes to whoever wins the item. Master loot, trade windows and drops per raid boss are unknown.

Camping, and the Greenhouse

Camping is a profession-like system, not a new profession slot. A Basic Campfire makes a campsite; characters resting there can use each other’s facilities and get temporary buffs, each character can add a feature, and advanced features come from skill and from dungeon Blueprints. Some camp buffs, such as a Blacksmith’s Strength, a Tailor’s Spirit and an Herbalist’s Intellect, don’t stack with the matching class buff, so classes keep their value.

The Greenhouse is real: it’s Herbalism’s second-tier feature and grows herbs from planted seeds. It isn’t a separate gardening profession.

Merchant’s Favor, and the market

Forever turns the old supply-crate idea into the Azeroth Commerce Authority: Waylaid Crates turn in for Merchant’s Favor, which buys recipes, profession certifications and other goods. On the beta, Alchemy’s Friendly recipes cost 120 Favor, Honored ones usually 180, higher ones 240, and its 300-skill certification 1,000. Favor also buys the Pack Kodo mount (6,000) and the Minimule companion, and Legacy’s Performance Bonus raises the Favor each crate pays.

The Stranglethorn Fishing Extravaganza returns with a new Extravagant Extravaganza Coin. Wowhead’s fishing guide describes the first 50 Master Anglers each winning the pole and a coin on Sundays, 2–4 PM; Blizzard hasn’t confirmed the final schedule. The Black Market Auction House at Powderfuse Port exists, but its full stock and rotation are unknown.

Crafted mounts aren’t a known launch feature: the mount data has no new Engineering mount. The Engineering companion pets remain; they’re in the companions book.

The interface

Modernized without becoming Retail: gamepad support, a choice of HD or SD character models, optional transmog, and a character panel whose tooltips explain combat stats. Blizzard is restricting computational addons, the kind that solve encounters, while keeping addon support; the exact API was still moving in the beta. There’s no confirmed built-in damage meter, and no announced change to bag, bank or quest-log sizes.

Legacy

One of Forever’s biggest departures. Challenges earn Legacy Points for the whole Battle.net account, and each character spends the pool on perks. Launch has 65 challenges worth 65 points, but a character can spend only 16 at first.

Perks include vendor discounts, more Merchant’s Favor, help with profession skill-ups, extra gathering yields, longer food and buff durations, faster and cheaper flights, better rested XP, reputation gains, less durability loss, more Honor and reagent conveniences. Cosmetic milestones: the Replica Ironforge Air Rifle toy, the Spectral Bear Cub at 25 points, the Spectral Bear Tabard, and the Reins of the Spectral Bear at 55. Legacy and Collections are account-wide across every ruleset; Hardcore, when it comes, is one-way, its progress flowing out to the account but nothing flowing in.

Rulesets, not realms

Forever’s characters live in realmless ruleset ecosystems: Normal, PvP, Roleplaying and Roleplay-PvP at launch, with Hardcore later. Dungeon and raid groups form within one ruleset, while battleground matchmaking can cross rulesets except Hardcore. The PvP ecosystem is faction-locked; Normal and Roleplaying allow both factions on one account. Cross-faction parties still don’t exist.

the honor system, rebuilt

PvP

The ranks survive, Private to Grand Marshal, but not the weekly race against the rest of the server.

Vanilla’s ranks came from weekly standings, with decay pushing everyone to keep grinding. Forever splits them into two tracks: Honor, a currency spent at vendors, and Rank Points, which advance rank. A seasonal Field of Honor Journey adds persistent tasks; some of its milestones award Legacy Points, and missed seasons stay completable. Battleground Honor is normalized so no single battleground is mandatory.
