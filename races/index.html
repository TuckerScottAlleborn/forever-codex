The Races

Racial weapon skill is gone for good. A race now picks a kind of power, not a weapon.

Vanilla’s Alliance race choice ran through Human weapon skill and a handful of resistances. Forever retires both on purpose. Blizzard says weapon specializations stay gone so that future dungeon and raid loot isn’t judged by a race’s “required” weapon type.

What’s left is sharper and more active: Human control-breaking and Spirit, Dwarf hit and defense, Night Elf timed crit and stealth, Gnome resources and escapes, and Skyborne sustained haste and traversal. For most specs no race is wrong, but the choices are now about whether progression hit, timed burst, sustained throughput, PvP control answers, personal defense or leveling convenience matters most.

Human

Will to Survive · Perception · Ruthless · The Human Spirit

verdict

The safest Alliance pick for a character that will PvP a lot, and a coherent Spirit-healing Priest. Human lost the old reason to be mandatory for melee PvE and gained a much stronger control-breaking identity.

The Human SpiritPassive

leveling

Losing Diplomacy is the biggest quality-of-life nerf against Vanilla: no automatic 10% on every reputation grind. In exchange, +10% Spirit trims downtime for classes that actually lean on Spirit regeneration, and the stun break helps in dangerous questing and world PvP.

dungeons and raids

+1% crit is universal and weapon-agnostic, but a Human Warrior, Rogue or Ret Paladin no longer gets anything like the boss-level weight of +5 weapon skill. Dwarf hit, Night Elf timed crit and Skyborne haste are now real competitors, which is the outcome Blizzard says it wanted.

pvp

Perception keeps its answer to Rogue and Druid openers; Will to Survive adds an instant stun escape. Two separate common problems, answered without giving up a combat passive. Gnome is still better into roots and snares, and Dwarf into Bleeds, Poisons and Diseases.

The offensive passive went from a beta Mace Specialization to Earth Fury (+2% crit damage and healing) on October 16, then to +1% hit on October 26.

The strangest late-beta journey of any Alliance race, ending somewhere very useful: universal hit, the cleanest defensive cooldown, and the only Alliance Shaman.

StoneformRacial

Instant3 min cooldown

Removes Bleed, Poison and Disease effects and grants immunity to them. Physical damage taken reduced by 10% for 8 sec.

ChangedClassic gave +10% Armor instead. Flat reduction doesn’t depend on how armor interacts with a given hit.

Artisan’s PrecisionPassive

+1% chance to hit with spells and attacks.

NewAdded October 26, replacing Earth Fury.

Big Game HunterPassive

+5% damage against Beasts.

NewTakes over Dwarf Hunter flavor from +5 Gun skill.

Find TreasureTracking

Tracks nearby treasure alongside other tracking, with a chance of extra items from most chests.

ImprovedClassic competed with normal tracking. No proc rate has been published.

leveling

Much better than the raw Classic list suggests. +1% hit trims frustrating misses, +5% against Beasts covers a large share of open-world mobs, Find Treasure can turn up real extra loot, and Stoneform is a genuine emergency button.

dungeons and raids

Until a hit requirement is met, Artisan’s Precision is straight reliability and throughput; past it, the hit can still free an item or talent point for something stronger, and is only wasted when it can’t be converted. The most complete tank package for Protection Warrior and Paladin. Hit does nothing for healing.

pvp

Extremely matchup-dependent and extremely good in the matchups it answers. Rogues are the obvious case: Poison and Bleed removal plus a Physical reduction window go straight at their pressure. Stoneform does nothing for a stun, which is why Dwarf and Human aren’t interchangeable PvP races.

dwarf shaman

Dwarf is the only Alliance Shaman race at launch, so Alliance Shaman has no racial decision to make. The kit is genuinely useful anyway: Elemental and Enhancement use the hit directly, all three specs get Stoneform, and Enhancement occasionally gets Big Game Hunter. The Shaman sheet.

The playmaker’s race: burst timing, movement and stealth, with the best Alliance “press during the burn” racial and real Hunter, Feral and Discipline synergy. Not a source of Retail-style Shadowmeld restealth.

Burst crit plus mobility and stealth: one of the most obviously Classic-plus redesigns.

Elune’s LightRacial

Instant3 min cooldown

+10% critical strike chance with all spells and attacks for 15 sec.

NewClassic had nothing like it.

ShadowmeldRacial

Instant10 sec cooldown · 2 min in combat

Stealth while stationary. Used in combat, it discourages enemies from attacking.

ChangedClassic was out-of-combat stealth only. In combat it works like Fade, not Vanish: no Stealth-required abilities.

QuicknessPassive

+1% dodge and +2% movement speed, stacking with other speed effects. Night Elf Rogues and Druids are detected in stealth as if one level higher.

ExpandedClassic had the dodge; the movement speed is new.

Wisp SpiritPassive

+75% movement speed while dead.

RaisedFrom Classic’s +50%.

leveling

+2% always-on movement looks tiny on a character sheet and compounds over dozens of hours. Elune’s Light is a real “kill this elite now” button, Shadowmeld keeps its open-world safety uses, and corpse runs get faster in a Classic-shaped world.

dungeons and raids

Fifteen seconds of every 180 is a crude 0.83 points of average crit when used on cooldown, which understates it whenever a short fight, burn phase or major cooldown lets the whole window land where it matters. It boosts healing crits too, and Hunter pets now inherit the player’s crit, so the window reaches the pet.

pvp

Especially strong on Rogue and Druid because it stacks several relevant pieces: harder-to-detect stealth, extra speed, combat Shadowmeld and a large crit window. Human, Gnome and Dwarf have cleaner hard counters to stuns, roots and poisons; Night Elf is about initiative and playmaking.

The race that feels least like Vanilla numbers shuffled around. Every part of the kit is more deliberate.

Eureka!Racial

Instant2 min cooldown

For 8 sec, the class’s damaging abilities cost 10% less and deal 10% more damage. The Priest version also covers healing and Power Word: Shield.

NewA “next three abilities” charge model until October 16. Non-periodic damage only.

Escape ArtistRacial

Instant2 min cooldown

Removes movement-impairing effects and grants immunity to them for 3 sec.

ChangedClassic was a cast on a shorter cooldown, with no immunity window.

Expansive MindPassive

+5% maximum Mana, Energy or Rage, by class.

ChangedClassic gave +5% Intellect, with no Energy or Rage version.

Engineering SpecializationPassive

Engineering devices fail or backfire 20% less often.

ChangedClassic gave +15 Engineering skill: early recipes then, reliability now.

leveling

Exceptionally coherent for resource-using solo classes. More resource, a real burn cooldown, an emergency root and snare escape and fewer Engineering mishaps all pay off before raid gear matters.

dungeons and raids

Mage, Warlock and the new Gnome Priest stand out: a bigger reservoir plus a window of cheaper, stronger casts. Eureka’s 8 of every 120 seconds is 6.67% uptime, roughly 0.67% on the damage it touches, so comparing it with an always-on passive by tooltip alone misleads. It favors direct casts; DoT ticks aren’t boosted, so Affliction gets less.

pvp

Still the anti-movement-control race, now more all-in per press: instant, two minutes, three seconds of immunity. A 20% cut to Engineering failures matters in organized PvP. Eureka! can’t be used in stealth or while invisible, per the October 16 notes, so it’s no Rogue opener.

With Eureka (now counting Power Word: Shield), +5% maximum Mana and Escape Artist, current Discipline and Holy guidance puts Gnome among the strongest-looking Alliance Priests.

leveling

Walk on Air takes terrain lines ordinary Classic characters can’t comfortably take, though ten seconds and a cooldown keep it far from flight. Read Leyline trims downtime anywhere, and more so once Attuned.

dungeons and raids

Potentially the cleanest sustained offensive passive on the Alliance, but 2% haste isn’t 2% DPS: it depends on how much of a spec responds to attack and cast speed under Classic-derived timing. Mage is the cleanest fit, Hunter uses ranged haste, Warrior and Rogue melee haste, and Druid both. Elemental Insight’s raid value depends on encounter rosters.

pvp

No stun break, cleanse or root escape. Skyborne’s edge is speed of execution, glide positioning and sustain, so other races lean ahead for PvP unless Walk on Air or the class’s own control enables a particular strategy.

read leyline, before and after

Most criticism before October 26 was about finding Ley Lines at all, in dungeons, battlegrounds and some zones. October 16 added spawn locations; October 26’s one-hour Attuned state travels with the character, even through death. Older complaints describe a version that isn’t the launch design, and the final version has barely been tested by anyone yet.

who can play one

Blizzard gates the Skyborne race, and the Zephras Isle starting questline, behind the Skyborne Heroic Pack or higher.

field notes

Tooltip checks

The racial assumptions most likely to mislead a veteran, or to survive in a guide written before the last two passes.

tooltip checkDoesn’t hold

“Shadowmeld is a second Vanish.”

In combat it works like Fade: threat and targeting relief, no access to Stealth-required abilities. Probably the edge case most likely to betray Retail muscle memory.

tooltip checkDoesn’t hold

“Eureka is +10% damage on everything for eight seconds.”

Only non-periodic damage, so DoT-heavy builds discount the headline. It also stopped being three charges on October 16 and gained Power Word: Shield support.

tooltip checkDoesn’t hold

“Alliance raids want Dwarf Priests for Fear Ward.”

Every Priest learns Fear Ward at level 20, and Devouring Plague is baseline too.

tooltip checkStale

“Human gets 2% crit.”

That was October 16. The launch target is +1% crit with +10% Spirit; the general race guides still show 2% and 5%.

tooltip checkStale

“Dwarf is the crit-damage race.”

Earth Fury lasted ten days. Dwarf theorycraft written between October 16 and 26 is already obsolete: it’s +1% hit now.

tooltip checkStale

“Skyborne get 3% haste, but need a Ley Line inside every instance.”

Both describe earlier builds. Launch is 2% haste, and the hour-long Attuned buff travels with the character.

tooltip checkUnspecified

“Find Treasure is worth so much gold an hour.”

The extra-loot proc is real, but no rate has been published, so there’s no responsible way to price it yet.

tooltip checkUnspecified

“Hunter pets don’t care about player racials.”

October 16 fixed pets to inherit the player’s higher melee or ranged crit, and class testing treats Elune’s Light as pet burst. How much it adds at 60 is still unmeasured.

the timeline is part of the answer

Tuning history

  1. Sep 12
    Forever revealed

    The Skyborne and the broad direction for updated racials, alongside Forever as a permanent level-60 branch.

  2. Sep 22
    Race and class combinations

    High Order and Windshaper Skyborne split by faction; Human Hunter, Gnome Priest and Dwarf Shaman highlighted for the Alliance.

  3. Sep 30
    Priest deep dive

    Fear Ward and Devouring Plague made baseline; Gnome’s Confounding Flash and Contingency Plan, Human Divine Grace and Dwarf Chastise introduced; Night Elf Starshards and Elune’s Grace changed. Divine Grace and Contingency Plan have since gone from 5 to 10 minute cooldowns.

  4. Oct 8
    Another pass signaled

    Lead developer commentary after community concern about strong active racials and weapon-skill disparities.

  5. Oct 16
    The first major pass
    • Human Sword Specialization becomes Ruthless, +2% crit
    • Dwarf’s beta Mace Specialization becomes Earth Fury; Find Treasure gains bonus chest loot
    • Wind-Blessed 1% → 3%; Walk on Air to a 90 sec cooldown; Read Leyline usable while moving, with more Ley Lines
    • Eureka becomes an 8 sec window with no charges, and supports Power Word: Shield
    • Quickness’s movement speed made to stack
  6. Oct 26
    The final pre-launch pass
    • Ruthless 2% → 1%; The Human Spirit 5% → 10%
    • Earth Fury removed; Artisan’s Precision, +1% hit, added
    • Wind-Blessed 3% → 2%; Read Leyline redesigned around 15 sec of +50% regeneration and an hour-long Attuned state at +100%

    Blizzard framed it as prioritizing racial identity without racials so strong they override player preference, and kept weapon specializations out.

  7. Nov 4
    Launch

    After a pass only nine days earlier, every absolute “best race” ranking is provisional until level-60 data exists.

the bottom line

Choosing a race

Human

When PvP is a major priority, or for a Spirit-using Priest. The old melee-PvE mandate is gone; a control-breaking identity replaces it.

Dwarf

For the strongest-looking progression and tank package, anyone who expects to care about hit, Stoneform, Alliance Shaman, or racial power that’s dependable rather than tied to a burst window.

Night Elf

For burst timing, movement, stealth, Hunter and Feral synergy, or the most playmaking Alliance kit, without expecting Retail-style Shadowmeld restealth.

Gnome

For the most coherent caster and Priest package, Engineering-heavy PvP, or a Warrior or Rogue for whom breaking roots and snares defines the class.

Skyborne

For an always-on offensive passive over a burst button, especially on Mage, Druid or Hunter, or for traversal. The 2% haste is promising and the least proven.

the conclusion

Vanilla asked which weapon a race was good with. Forever asks which kind of power a character should carry.

Human control-breaking and Spirit, Dwarf hit and defense, Night Elf timed crit and stealth, Gnome resources, cooldowns and escapes, Skyborne sustained haste and traversal. The tuning is close enough, and new enough, that for most specs no race is defensibly wrong, but the trade-offs are sharper than they have ever been on the Alliance.
